  1. Jensen Huang has been reinstated as NVIDIA CEO effective immediately, triggering a >30% surge in the stock and a broad >10% rally across technology shares; the market's violent dependence on a single executive's employment status underscores how fragile confidence in the AI trade has become.

  3. The core disconnect: AI capex spending was responsible for roughly half of S&P 500 earnings growth in 2026, but the industry has yet to demonstrate meaningful monetization of AI investments beyond selling the picks and shovels; without proof of ROI at the application layer, investors question whether the infrastructure buildout can sustain itself.
  4. November has been catastrophic for growth stocks; the Nasdaq is down 11.1% since November 1, the semiconductor index (SOX) is off 21.2%, and NVIDIA itself has fallen 38.4% from May highs above $220 to Friday's close near $126, though today's reinstatement news has reversed much of that damage.
  5. S&P 500 has entered correction territory down 10%+ from its peak, while the Nasdaq-100 briefly entered bear market territory intraday; the S&P 500's 2026 gains have compressed to just 0.8%, putting the full-year return within 0.5% of zero.
  6. Three pre-election developments crushed sentiment: voters overwhelmingly rejected AI-skeptical ballot measures and candidates, weakening Democratic support for the AI build-out; NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's earlier resignation amid investigations signaled governance crisis; and Anthropic's record-sized IPO failed to stabilize sentiment and continued to slide, suggesting valuation exhaustion even among new entrants.
  7. Goldman Sachs expects S&P 500 earnings growth to decelerate from roughly 30% in 2026 to 11% in 2027, with stock returns moderating accordingly; this marks a structural shift from rapid expansion to what Goldman calls "positive but decelerating growth," historically associated with rotation away from concentrated mega-cap winners.
  8.
