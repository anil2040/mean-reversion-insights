  3. The U.S. dollar has surged to a 20-month high, reflecting both safe-haven flows as stocks wobble and the structural pull of higher rates and AI-driven foreign capital inflows, though strategists warn the rally may fade if AI slowdown eventually weakens the broader economy and forces the Fed into retreat.
  4. Anthropic's IPO has fallen below its offering price in an unusually swift decline, poisoning confidence in AI monetization and raising questions about whether a cohort of AI-focused companies can justify their capital and investor expectations, with OpenAI's own public offering prospects now clouded.

  6. Third-quarter GDP was revised sharply upward to 4.3% annualized from 3.4%, exceeding historical norms for such revisions and demonstrating economy-wide strength, though inflation on multiple gauges (CPI, PCE, core readings) remains persistently above 2.9%, forcing the Fed to stay restrictive.
  7. Consumer spending remains resilient as evidenced by expectations for 6.7% holiday sales growth year-over-year, with affluent households (80th to 98th percentile) now driving four times the spending of the poorest households compared to three times in the mid-1980s, making wealth effects on stock holdings an outsized driver of near-term demand.
  8. Black Friday online sales data and retail-sector performance today will serve as a real-time barometer of mass-affluent health and willingness to spend despite September stock volatility, with the mass-affluent's consumption particularly tied to confidence in AI and technology stock valuations.
  9. Nvidia's return to leadership under Jensen Huang sparked a 31.5% single-day rally Monday that collapsed into a 5.9% drop Tuesday, epitomizing the violent swings plaguing tech; valuations are now 5% cheaper than the S&P 500 on average versus a 20% premium over the past decade, raising the question of whether value has finally emerged in a sector in secular correction mode.
  10. Watch for resolution of two overlapping risks next week: the November employment report will test whether the 4.1% unemployment and 90,000 expected job additions can sustain consumer spending momentum, and the evolving interpretation of AI capex spending (whether growth simply moderates to still-elevated levels or suffers genuine slowdown) will determine if 2027 brings broadening leadership or continued concentration.
  11. DASHBOARD READ: The market is caught between two competing signals. Inflation remains stubbornly above target on all four measures, credit spreads have widened significantly from their cycle lows, and the Shiller CAPE at 36.4x remains in the top percentile of history, all consistent with an overheated Macro Heat Score of 74. Yet the Fed is tightening into what it believes is a resilient 4.3% annualized growth economy with unemployment steady at 4.1%, and markets are pricing certainty of further rate hikes despite a corroding valuation picture in technology. The VIX at 24.8 reflects not complacency but active caution; the broad rally today is real but narrow (tech rebounding, defensives lagging), and the dollar's strength shows investors remain structurally bullish on U.S. returns, not emotional optimism. For a deep-value investor, this is the transition moment: the AI-driven concentration trade is breaking, valuations are recompressing toward historical norms in some sectors, credit is pricing real risks that equities are ignoring, and the economy remains strong enough to avoid recession but tight enough that mean reversion in multiples is the likelier path than multiple expansion into 2027.
